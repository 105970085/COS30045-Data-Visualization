Televisions and Energy Use

Televisions are common household appliances in Australia. Their electricity consumption can vary depending on the screen size, display technology, brightness and how long the television is used each day.

Data Story: Screen Size vs. Energy Consumption

Televisions are one of the most energy-consuming appliances in households. The scatter plot below shows each television model as a data point. The horizontal axis represents screen size, while the vertical axis represents labelled annual energy consumption (kWh/year).

As the plot demonstrates, larger screens consistently consume more electricity per year. This means that choosing a bigger TV not only increases the purchase cost but also leads to higher electricity bills over time.

Tips for Reducing Television Energy Use

Insight: Bigger TVs = higher annual energy use.
Recommendation: Consumers should balance screen size with energy efficiency. Choosing a smaller or more efficient model can save hundreds of kWh annually, lowering costs and reducing environmental impact.
